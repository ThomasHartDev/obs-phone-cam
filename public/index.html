obs-phone-cam

iPhone camera and iPad whiteboard as OBS sources. Same Wi-Fi, no app to install.

Camera controls (this laptop)

Leave the phone on the stand. Mirror, flip, rotate, filters, resolution, and mic all live here. OBS keeps the Browser Source; this page only talks to the phone.

    …

    Set the Browser Source Width and Height to match your canvas: 1080×1920 for vertical (TikTok/IG), 1920×1080 for landscape (YouTube). Then right-click the source → Transform → Fit to screen. A wide box with black bars means the source size doesn’t match the canvas. The URL is plain http (OBS can’t open the https one).

    3. Draw on an iPad

    Safari on iPad cannot share another app’s screen, so this page is the drawing surface (Excalidraw-style pen, shapes, Apple Pencil). Keep it open while you draw. The iPhone camera keeps running on its own OBS source.

    Size that source to your canvas (1920×1080 is a good start). Paper Green (chroma) on the iPad lets you key the drawing over the camera in OBS. On a laptop, Share window can send a real Excalidraw tab if the browser supports it.

    4. Use it as a camera device

    In OBS, right side under Controls, click Start Virtual Camera.

    Your phone now shows up as the camera named “OBS Virtual Camera” in Zoom, Teams, Discord, Chrome, or any app’s camera list. (OBS needs to stay open while you use it.)
